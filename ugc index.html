How a project moves from idea to finished video

Each step has a job. Skipping one is how brands end up with videos that look good and sell nothing.

  1. Product and brand researchLearn what is sold, to whom, and what the brand already says.
  2. Customer insight developmentFind what customers want, fear and doubt. Mark each point as fact or hypothesis.
  3. Competitor and market researchSee what others show and say, and where the gap is.
  4. Creative angle selectionPick a few different angles, not one idea reworded.
  5. Hook and script developmentWrite the opening seconds and the full script around one clear message.
  6. Video production planningPlan scenes, presenter, voiceover, captions and format.
  7. Content productionCreate the AI-assisted videos from the approved plan.
  8. Review and revisionsYou give feedback, I fix errors and refine. Facts get checked.
  9. Final deliveryOrganised files, scripts, captions and a clear handover.
  10. Evaluation and closureIf you share results, we review them honestly and plan the next test.

How I document a project

Every project gets the same structure. This example is a concept project, not a real campaign.

Concept case study: skincare serum ritual (sample brand)
  • Context: a sample serum brand selling online to adults with a simple routine.
  • Objective: make a short video that shows how easy the product is to use.
  • Insight (hypothesis): people may trust a routine more when they can picture doing it every day.
Angles, hooks and script
  • Angle A, the 30-second routine: hook "My whole routine takes 30 seconds."
  • Angle B, the doubt: hook "I thought serums were a waste of money."
  • Angle C, the shelf check: hook "Three things I look for in a serum."

Script outline: hook (0-3s), the problem, product shown in use, [benefit verified with the client], simple call to action. Product claims are only added after the client confirms them.

Testing recommendations
  • Compare the three hooks with the same video body, one change at a time.
  • Run each long enough to judge on thumb-stop rate and watch time, not on a single day.
